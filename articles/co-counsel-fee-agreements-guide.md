When two firms work a case together, the fee agreement is where trust either holds or breaks. Get it right up front and the partnership runs smoothly. Leave it vague and you're negotiating in the middle of a settlement.

## The basics

A co-counsel fee agreement spells out how the contingency fee is divided between the referring firm and the firm handling the case. The key terms:

- **Split percentage.** Who gets what share of the fee.
- **Work or responsibility basis.** Many jurisdictions require the split to reflect either proportional work or joint responsibility for the case.
- **Client consent.** The client must be informed of and agree to the fee-sharing arrangement, in writing.

## Where firms get burned

The most common failure is an undocumented handshake split. Months later, memories differ, the case settles for more (or less) than expected, and there's no clean record. The second is ignoring jurisdiction-specific ethics rules on fee division.

## The takeaway

Put the split, the basis, and the client consent in writing at the start. A clear agreement protects the relationship as much as the fee.
